Reading a rating

How ratings work

Every chain gets two ratings from 0 to 100, one for TradFi and one for AI. Each number falls into one of five named tiers, and each rating has its own tier names and two-letter codes. This page explains what the tiers mean, how gates can cap a rating, and what the categories measure.

TradFi tiers AI tiers Gates Categories

01The tiers

The two ratings answer different questions, so they never share tier names or codes. A badge always abbreviates the tier name printed next to it: EI is Emerging Institutional, a TradFi tier, and IR is Integration Ready, an AI tier, even though both use the same color. Only the five color bands and their order are shared between the two ratings.

TradFi tiers

Can an institution hold and use this asset today?

IGInstitutional Grade80–100Meets the bar an institution would set today, with clear legal status, regulated access, real usage and no structural weakness serious enough to block an allocation.
EIEmerging Institutional60–79On the institutional track but not there yet, so it may already have real institutional use while concentration, supply structure, regulation or confidentiality gaps still hold it back.
RGRetail Grade40–59Usable and held by individuals, but missing enough of the legal, access or structural basics that most institutions would not hold it today.
SPSpeculative20–39Thin fundamentals relative to the risks, so holding it is mostly a bet on future change rather than a judgment on what exists now.
HRHigh Risk0–19Serious structural, legal or security problems that rule it out for a careful institution today.

AI tiers

Can an autonomous agent safely transact on this chain today?

PGProduction Grade80–100An agent can rely on it in production today, with final settlement, enough programmability, a clean operating record and the tooling to build on it.
IRIntegration Ready70–79Ready to build on now, but with known limitations an agent has to design around.
EXExperimental55–69Agents can run on it, but gaps in finality, reliability, tooling or cost predictability make it suited to testing and limited exposure rather than production.
POPrototype Only40–54Fine for proofs of concept, but an agent should not be trusted with real value or real workloads here.
NVNot Viable0–39Missing basics an autonomous agent needs, so it is not a realistic platform for agents today.
A tier reads from the published score, after any gate cap. A capped chain's tier reflects its weakest foundation, and the chain page shows what it would rate without the cap. Tiers describe structural readiness today, not adoption, price or roadmap.

02Gates can cap a rating

Before any category is scored, each rating runs three foundation checks called gates. A gate does not add points. It sets a ceiling, so a chain with a serious foundational gap cannot score its way past it with strength elsewhere.

✓ PASS

Nothing disqualifying is present. No ceiling applies.

! PARTIAL

A real gap exists, and the rating cannot rise above that gate's ceiling.

✕ FAIL

The gap is fundamental, and a lower ceiling applies.

When categories add up to more than a gate allows, the chain publishes at the ceiling and is marked capped. Its page shows the uncapped number and which gate binds. Passing every gate is a floor, not a guarantee: it means nothing disqualifying was found, not that the chain rates well.

TRADFI GATES
Quantum ResistanceWhether the chain has a real plan to migrate off cryptography that quantum computers could eventually break.
Regulatory ClarityWhether there is a legitimate, live regulatory path for institutional capital to enter this asset.
Security PostureAudit depth, bug bounty coverage, clean exploit history, and battle-testing over time.
AI GATES
Deterministic FinalityHow fast and how certain settlement is: can an agent trust a transaction is final, and how long does it take?
Programmability TierHow much custom logic an agent can actually deploy and run on this chain today.
Operational ReliabilityRecent uptime history: has the chain had a qualifying halt or partition, and how long ago. The ceiling rises with each clean month after an incident.

03Nine categories per rating

Each rating is built from nine categories, each scored 0 to 100 and weighted into the overall number. A category score carries the tier code of its own rating, so an IG beside a TradFi category means that category on its own scored in the Institutional Grade range. Category weights are not published.

04What never moves a rating

Testnet features and announced plans earn nothing until they ship to mainnet. Price, sentiment and narrative are out of scope. The two ratings are never blended into one number, because the gap between them is often the most useful thing a rating says. The principles behind all of this are set out in the project constitution.
